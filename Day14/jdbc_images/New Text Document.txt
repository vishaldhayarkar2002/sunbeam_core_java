1. Read Uncommitted (Dirty Reads)

Imagine a student starts writing notes in a library book with a pencil but hasn’t erased the wrong parts yet. 
Another student comes along, looks at the book, and copies those notes — 
even though the first student might later erase them.

You can see unfinalized changes that may later disappear.

Like copying notes that might be erased → dirty reads.

*************************************************************************

2. Read Committed (Non-repeatable Reads)

You go to the library, open a book, and read a chapter.
Later, you reopen the same chapter during the same visit, but meanwhile, 
another student has replaced that chapter with an updated version.

The second time you read, the content is different.

This is a non-repeatable read: you don’t see dirty (unfinished) work, but you can see changes between reads.

*******************************************

3. Repeatable Read (Phantom Reads)


You open a book and read Chapter 5. During your visit, 
no one can edit Chapter 5 — it will stay the same no matter how many times you check.
But, while you’re reading, another student adds a brand new Chapter 6 or removes a Chapter 4.

So, your Chapter 5 is safe and consistent → no dirty or non-repeatable reads.

But new chapters appear (phantoms) when you scan through the table of contents again.

*******************************************

4. Serializable
The library only lets one student use a book at a time. 
If you’re reading, no one else can read or add/change chapters until you’re completely done.

Transactions are executed one after another, like standing in line.

Absolutely no dirty reads, non-repeatable reads, or phantom reads.

But it’s the slowest, since students have to wait.


************
Read Uncommitted: Copying notes someone hasn’t finalized.

Read Committed: Reading the same chapter twice, but it changes between reads.

Repeatable Read: Chapter stays the same, but new chapters might appear.

Serializable: Only one person can use the book at a time — total isolation.
